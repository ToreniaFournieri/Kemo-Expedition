### What helped

- Stopped an unproductive opening after 20 sorties with zero XP and revised the party. Protected attack-count equipment and used SEMI equipment automation to preserve useful builds.
- Matched the growing item pool with ranged and magic attackers, then used progressively deeper farming limits. A failed full-depth diagnostic identified heavy incoming magic damage; support classes and formation were adjusted using public battle logs and simulations.
- Rejected a risky fifth-floor farm after 16 defeats in 50 sorties. Returned to safer fourth-floor farming with Restoration. Including the fourth-floor elite improved XP yield sufficiently to reach party level 10.
- At level 10, locked the equipped items, used FULL to fill additional capacity, then returned to SEMI. The resulting forecast cleared 829 of 1,000 trials. Ranged attack count rose from 5 to 13, and the two main magic attackers rose from 6 to 14 and 5 to 12 attacks.
- Switched to individual full-depth sorties: the first reached room 23 and retreated wounded; the second cleared all 24 rooms. The winning battle ended with 843 of 1,468 HP remaining.

The forecast was an estimate, not a guarantee. These observations suggest improving the next opening by reaching productive farming earlier and reducing repeated build experiments; they do not establish an optimal score.

### Convenience findings

- The official launcher successfully created a fresh isolated Orca session and private connection handoff. Authenticated readiness checking worked without UI gameplay or DevTools.
- The automatic report supplies the padded score, mode, final six-character status table, configuration, winning operation, and ledger. Terminal observation exposes no legal gameplay actions and marks both normal sorties and Gods battles unavailable.
- Filling capacity while preserving existing equipment required lock, FULL, and SEMI configuration steps. A documented recipe or a future explicit fill-empty-slots operation could make this easier without changing equipment-selection rules.
- The custom client encountered terminal canonical-input limits for long JSON requests. Fixing its terminal input mode resolved this before requests reached the API. A reference client using pipes or request files would avoid this client-side launch inconvenience.
- Planned depth returns should be interpreted using the explicit return reason; legacy aggregate outcome labels alone can be misleading.
